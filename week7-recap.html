The other ledger
The Champ, +2.09

5-2 on a 32-45 all-play — the biggest gap between record and performance this paper has recorded in either league. Dave McD. is fourth in the table and seventh in the power rankings, and the simulator below has opinions about which one is right.

Pedantic lost. After six weeks of winning with ten men, an empty slot or a quarterback listed out, Neal finally met a team that scored 133.88 and had nothing left to answer with: 108.32, his second-lowest number of the season, and the unbeaten run ends at six. The Raleighwood Boo Bears did it with Dak Prescott, who was on the Waiver Wire Wizards' roster on Wednesday, was traded on Thursday morning and scored 23.28 on debut, and with 21.7 from Luther Burden III, who arrived from the same team nine days earlier.

The bigger number was elsewhere. Raleighwood Chargers 173.28, Poop Poop 148.52 — 321.80 points in one game, the most this league has ever produced and ten clear of the previous mark. Eric got 30.0 from Blake Cashman at linebacker, 23.5 from Bucky Irving and 21.9 from CeeDee Lamb four days after dropping Terry McLaurin and signing a second kicker. Ben's 148.52 is the second-biggest score anybody has lost with in the history of this competition, and he is 2-5.

The Real Chargers won again. Collin was 0-5 and twenty-six points short of a league record for futility three weeks ago; he has now scored 145.60 and 121.58 in consecutive weeks and won both by a combined 107 points. Tony Pollard's 35.0 was the best individual line of the week and Alex Singleton added 26.5 from linebacker. Forsaken Dave, on the other side of it, managed 85.82 — the lowest score of the week — and is 1-6 with 989.54 points conceded, which is a league record and sixty-four clear of anybody else.

This paper's team is 5-2 and lost the points lead by 8.14. 116.10 was enough against the Wizards, with Smith-Njigba's 26.8 the fourth-best line in the league for a third consecutive week, but the Boo Bears' 133.88 moved Ian to 973.96 against the editor's 965.82. Ian also has the best all-play record in the competition at 55-22 and the only roster in it this column could find nothing wrong with. On Sunday the editor is 0.8 points away from first place and has Malik Nabers on his bench, which is a sentence that will read very badly by Tuesday.

Two things for the record book.
